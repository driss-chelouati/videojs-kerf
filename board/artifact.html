System

The numbers behind the cut.

Outer radius = inner radius + frame: 20 = 14 + 6. The time tab's base fillet and its top corner share a 10 px radius, so the step reads as one cut.

Motion

Rise
200 ms, cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1). Scales from 0.94 out of its trigger.
Sink
120 ms ease-in, a fade with a 4 px drop. Faster out than in.
Interrupted
Transitions, not keyframes, so a menu closed mid-rise turns back from where it is.
Band
Sinks 240 ms after 2 s idle while playing. Any pointer move or key brings it back.
Reduced
Every change is instant. Tabs still appear at their trigger, so the link stays clear.

Access

Contrast
Controls never sit on footage. Ink on frame 17:1 dark, 18:1 light; secondary text above 7:1.
Focus
2 px ink ring with a 2 px cut on every control, menu item and slider blade.
Keyboard
Space, arrows, M, F, C. Esc closes a menu and returns focus to its button.
Transparency
Nothing to remove: every surface is already solid.
Forced colours
Frame Canvas, ink CanvasText, fill and focus Highlight.

Design thinking

Most players float their controls over the picture, then fight the picture for contrast with gradients and blur. Kerf cuts the picture instead. The controls live in a notch of the player's own frame, a solid surface in the host product's colour, so they read the same over a white sky or a black night.

Everything that opens (the scrub preview, menus, volume, tooltips, errors) rises out of that frame from the control that asked for it, and sinks back when the film plays. Transient feedback hangs from the top edge. One radius system, one accent, the official solid icons, tabular time. Familiar to use, unmistakable to look at.
